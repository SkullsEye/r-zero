The decision layer changed, and the old number went with it. A calibrated sequential test used to make the block decision, and on held-out data it put 2.265% of innocent customers' transactions on the floor — 1,172 of them from a single account, because an automatic block with no human behind it never ends. Matched on the same recall, the review desk stops 0.234% and its worst case is 5. That is 9.7× less damage and a 234× smaller worst case, and none of it is calibrated: the ceiling is your desk capacity and the damage is capped by your review latency.

Why the browser model is smaller on purpose. It uses only the six columns anyone can supply — time, amount, card, address, email, device — so it reaches PR-AUC 0.208 where the full model reaches 0.466. On the 30 days it streams above it scores 0.217, against 0.186 for the identical model with the contagion features deleted. Every number it shows you is computed in front of you.
